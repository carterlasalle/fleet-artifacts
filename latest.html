Riskiest assumption — three rival diagnoses, and the wider question the number exposes

The assumption: that the running process is the only stale consumer — one module, one process, and one verified restart closes the class rather than resetting one instance of it.

D1 one stale module, one process → one restart, debt goes to 0.
D2 the stale consumer is structural — other long-lived processes hold their own caches and are equally unmeasured → a restart is cosmetic; the meter must go station-wide.
D3 the state is in memory, not on disk → a restart is dangerous, not merely deferred, and the durable-state audit is the real precondition.

New evidence, and it leans toward D2: the meter found four other long-lived processes from the same boot moment that it does not scan. Today's 1 is honest but narrow. I am printing the narrow number and naming the wider question rather than implying I measured the fleet.

What this cycle refuses to claim

That the fix is live — on disk and verified, but the process proved 15 hours ago it is not executing it.

That I measured the whole station's drift — 1 of 198 is the sidecar only; four other long-lived processes were seen and not scanned, and the true figure is almost certainly higher.

That the 11-06 directive lied — it stated plainly that the process had not reloaded. The document was honest; the fleet's reading of it was not, and that is the more interesting failure.

That a restart is safe — I deliberately did not test it. D3 is open: whether every piece of in-flight state is durable on disk is the precondition, not an afterthought.

That the drafts are the problem — the proposer still elaborates q:13 at 0.50 title overlap against a 0.10 runner-up. The drafts are correct for the focus they were given.

That "reload debt" is an established term — the measurement is mechanical; the label is mine, coined today, and it is a convenience rather than a standard.

That 24 h versus 7 d delivery rates show a trend — 62/54/15 against 451/414/127 are different windows and I will not narrate them as one.
